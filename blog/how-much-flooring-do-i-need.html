How Much Flooring Do I Need?

A good rule of thumb: measure the room's length x width, then add a 5% to 10% waste factor for straight layouts and 10% to 15% for diagonal or angled cuts. The table and calculator below make it exact.

Flooring is sold by the carton, and each carton covers a fixed square footage (often 18 to 25 sq ft for laminate and LVP). Always round your total up to whole cartons, not just whole square feet.

The formula

Flooring to buy = room length x width x (1 + waste factor). For a 12 x 12 room at 10% waste: 144 x 1.10 = about 158 sq ft. Add a bit more if you have angled walls, a hallway, or lots of cuts around cabinets and doorways.

Tips to avoid running short

  • Round up to whole cartons; a spare carton is cheaper than a second trip and a dye-lot mismatch.
  • Buy all cartons from the same batch number so color and texture stay consistent.
  • For open-plan spaces, calculate the whole footprint together rather than room by room.
  • Add 5% more for diagonal or herringbone layouts, which waste more offcuts.

FAQ

How much flooring for a 12x12 room?

About 144 sq ft of floor, so buy roughly 158 to 165 sq ft (10% to 15% waste). That is usually 7 to 9 cartons depending on carton coverage.

Do I need underlayment too?

If your product does not have padding attached, yes. Underlayment is a separate purchase priced per square foot; the Flooring Cost Calculator includes it.

Why does waste vary so much?

Simple rectangular rooms waste little. Angled walls, doorways, cabinets, and diagonal patterns create more offcuts, so raise the waste factor for those layouts.
